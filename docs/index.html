Slow Equilibration and Persistent Stationary Correlations in Spherical Spin Glasses

P. M. Aronow &and Patrick Lopatto

The model

Two questions about one diffusion.

A centered Gaussian field on the sphere of radius √N defines a Gibbs measure π and reversible Langevin dynamics Pt. Its covariance is ξ(r) = Σp≥2 cprp, with cp ≥ 0. The paper studies both a uniform initial law and the joint behavior of two endpoints of the stationary dynamics.

E[HN(x)HN(y)] = Nξ(R(x,y))

L = ΔSN + β∇SNHN · ∇SN

R(x,y) = x · y / N   ·   π(dx) ∝ eβHN(x)νN(dx)

A(r) = (1 − r)ξ′(r)/r   ·   q* = sup{r ∈ (0,1) : β2A(r) > 1}

The mathematical scope

Six headline results.

These summaries accompany the complete statements and hypotheses in the manuscript and the Lean specification.

Theorem 1.2

Chen hypothesis

Slow equilibration from the uniform law

For a finite mixture of minimum degree at least four, under strict one-step replica symmetry breaking and a nondegenerate contact, the uniform start remains asymptotically at total variation distance one from equilibrium throughout an exponential time interval.

inf0 ≤ t ≤ exp(γN) ‖νNPt − π‖TV → 1

Convergence is in probability over the disorder; ξ and β are fixed.

Corollary 1.3

Chen hypothesis

Concrete strictly 1RSB classes

The hypotheses of Theorem 1.2 are verified for pure models of degree at least four above their static threshold, finite mixtures of minimum degree at least four with strictly convex ξ″−1/2, small even perturbations of pure even models of degree at least four, and an explicit nonconvex 4 + 12 example.

The manuscript states the temperature, degree, coefficient, and small-perturbation restrictions for each case.

Theorem 1.4

Chen hypothesis*

Other initial laws, with the same time exponent

Separation extends to disorder-independent initial laws, adaptive convex combinations of prescribed laws with KN = O(Nκ) and κ < (d − 2)/2, and disorder-dependent laws whose relative entropy with respect to νN is o(log N) in probability.

*The asymptotic conclusions retain Chen. The finite-dimensional Haar comparison has no literature premise.

Theorem 1.5

No literature premise

Five forms of stationary memory

For finite mixtures with βd < β < βs, every fixed level 0 < b < q*2 has exponentially many slow modes, positive centered-spin projection, and a persistent connected correlation. Most Gibbs starting points remain far from equilibrium, and stationary endpoint mutual information is at least cN.

For 0 < b < q*2, all five conclusions share an event of probability at least 1 − Ce−aN and an exponential time scale.

Theorem 1.6

No literature premise

Correlation at every level below q*

In the same finite-mixture regime, the centered-spin projection and stationary connected correlation reach every fixed level 0 < b < q*, with probability tending to one. The slow-mode count, typical-start separation, and information bound retain exponentially small failure probabilities.

The stronger plateau has no asserted probability rate or matching upper bound.

Theorem 1.7

No literature premise

Small eigenvalues near the dynamical threshold

For mixtures satisfying Σp≥2 p5/2√cp < ∞ and βd < βs, exponentially many eigenvalues remain small as β decreases to βd. The largest global maximizer qd of A has contact of order 2m, which determines the spectral exponent.

λ⌊exp(κ*N)⌋ ≤ exp[−(1 − ϑ)Cdε1+1/(2m)N]

Here ε = β2/βd2 − 1. For fixed 0 < ϑ < 1 and sufficiently small fixed ε > 0, the bound holds for large N with exponentially small failure probability. The count exponent κ* is independent of ε; the theorem concerns eigenvalues.

Formal statements and proofs

The hypotheses stay visible.

Universal pressure lower tails are proved in Lean for every finite mixture and every real β. No selected headline endpoint assumes a Subag result or a separate pressure-tail hypothesis.

Theorem 1.2, Corollary 1.3, and the asymptotic parts of Theorem 1.4 retain Chen’s finite-mixture Parisi formula as an explicit theorem parameter. The finite-dimensional Haar comparison and Theorems 1.5–1.7 have no literature premise. Each result retains its mathematical model, temperature, and regularity assumptions.

The independent Challenge states the selected claims, and the Solution supplies the proofs. The comparison configuration, exact dependencies, and verification records are part of the package.
